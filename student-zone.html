FORENSIC GLOSSARY

Search through our extensive database of forensic terminology.

Autopsy

A postmortem medical examination performed by a pathologist to discover the cause and manner of death, or the extent of disease.

Ballistics

The science of mechanics that deals with the flight, behavior, and effects of projectiles, especially bullets, gravity, and firearms.

CODIS

Combined DNA Index System; the FBI's program of support for criminal justice DNA databases as well as the software used to run these databases.

DNA

Deoxyribonucleic acid, the fundamental building block for an individual's entire genetic makeup. Highly individualizing evidence.

Fingerprint

An impression or mark made on a surface by a person's fingertip, unique to each individual and used for identification.

Locard’s Exchange Principle

The foundational forensic theory formulated by Edmond Locard stating that "every contact leaves a trace."

Luminol

Definition: A chemical that exhibits chemiluminescence.
Principle: Reacts with iron in hemoglobin.
Application: Detects trace or cleaned-up amounts of blood left at crime scenes.
Limitations: Can yield false positives with bleach, copper, or horseradish.

Postmortem Interval (PMI)

The amount of time that has elapsed since a person has died, often estimated using entomology or body temperature (algor mortis).

Rigor Mortis

The postmortem stiffening of the joints and muscles of a body a few hours after death, usually lasting from one to four days.

Toxicology

The branch of science concerned with the nature, effects, and detection of poisons, drugs, and alcohol in the body.

Trace Evidence

Small, often microscopic materials that could be transferred during the commission of a crime (e.g., hair, fibers, glass, paint chips).
